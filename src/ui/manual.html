📘 Field Manual

Plain-language rules for players. Every number on this page is read live from the running server's rulebook — house-rule a constant and the manual updates itself. Want the deep version — every order, the terrain matrix, all the math? Ask your GM for docs/GAME_GUIDE.md in the project.

The clock

Time moves in ticks of 6 minutes. When nothing is happening the engine fast-forwards (an hour or a watch at a time); the moment forces are near contact it drops to single turns. You plot orders whenever you like — they take effect on the next tick. Orders stand: a unit keeps doing the last thing you told it until you tell it something else. Better: plot a plan (the ➕ then… button) — a queue of steps that run in sequence, march there, dig in, rest — and give the unit ⚡ standing rules: if-then reflexes (when a contact closes within 2, blow the bridge) that persist across every order, fire even off-net, and interrupt whatever the unit is doing when they trip.

Seeing & being seen

You never see enemy units — you see contacts, which climb a ladder as sensors confirm them:

GHOSTsomething's out there — position ±1 hex, nothing else
SHADOWa track: heading and rough size (enough to plot a STRIKE or intercept)
CONTACTcomposition — what kinds of units
LOCKfull picture, down to the unit list

Contacts fade one level per hour without re-detection. Moving, firing, roads, and ACTIVE sensors make you louder; woods, night, rain, HIDE, and going EMCON DARK make you quieter. ECM raises the enemy's difficulty; probes and Mobile HQs see further; DECOY units read one size bigger than they are (close recon at LOCK sees the truth). Sharing a hex with the enemy is an automatic mutual LOCK.

The command net

Orders only reach formations on the net — within reach of a command node (your HQ, key facilities). The net chains: any Mobile HQ vehicle and any 📡 relay mast within 24 hexes of a node (or another chained relay) extends the umbrella another 12 hexes — drive a relay truck forward and the net follows your advance. The chain is fragile on purpose: a relay that goes dark, gets jammed by ECM, or dies drops everything downstream off the net, and relaying makes you easier to spot. Off-net units run on their standing orders and conditionals until someone physically reaches them; their reports come home when they do. Losing the HQ hurts more than losing a lance.

Ground operations

March speed is set by your slowest machine, from its real record sheet. Roads are fast; woods and rough are slow; FORCED_MARCH trades readiness for speed and CAUTIOUS trades speed for stealth. Moving breaks HIDE and DIG_IN. Minefields bite movers until engineers BREACH them; artillery FIREs at spotted hexes (Long Tom reaches 15 hexes — D-052 halved the mapsheet printings) but reveals its own firing position to counter-battery. The guns are graded by their real tubes, magazines run dry under sustained fire (REARM refills), and shells flatten buildings: a photographed enemy base is a surveyed grid you can bombard clean — a DESTROYED facility loses its guns, radar, net, runway, and stores. Readiness (0–10) drops when you fight, march hard, or starve — REST recovers it (2/pulse in supply). Rail moves a column at 12 hexes/hour — four times march pace; rivers are crossable only at bridges, and engineers can span one behind you (BUILD_BRIDGE) or blow one in the enemy's face (DEMOLISH — instant, and everyone hears it). On the map, click your units to select them (shift-click for a group order) and leave ✨ auto-route on: click a destination and the route plots itself with an arrival time, using only the terrain your side has scouted.

Supply & the economy

Every formation eats 1 SP/day (double after fighting), drawn along a road-favoring line from a depot or convoy. Cut off ⇒ readiness bleeds daily. Factories mint 2 SP/day while you hold them; convoys haul stock forward; off-world imports flow until the enemy holds both jump points (blockade). REARM refills ammo (1 SP/unit) at a depot, factory, spaceport, or convoy in your hex. REPAIR queues damaged units into the shop there — DAMAGED costs 1 SP and 1 day, CRIPPLED 2 SP and 3 days — and returns them with a clean record sheet.

Battles

The engine never rolls a battle. When forces collide the campaign freezes and hands the GM a briefing: entry edges, who deploys first, initiative (better intel = more; a C3 master adds 1), artillery in range, CAS overhead, reinforcement timing — and each unit's persisted record-sheet damage from earlier fights. You play it on the table; the marked-up cards come back as the result. Damage, ammo, pilots, salvage, and fuel all flow home; the clock resumes.

Careers, wounds & salvage

Named pilots earn XP for surviving (1), winning (+1), and kills (+2 each); every 8 XP the weaker skill improves, and 5 kills makes an ace. Wounds heal at 3 days per hit — or 1 with a MASH on your side. Wrecks you hold the field over become salvage: recover one intact (2d6 ≥ 8) and a refit (3 SP, 4 days) delivers it to your roster with a pool pilot in the seat. Air kills rain down onto the map below.

The sky

Fuel is the game — and now, so is position. Every ground hex has sky directly above it: you launch over your own base, fly to the mission hex by hex at your card's real speed (dash = Safe Thrust hexes per turn, cruise half that), and cross whatever radar lies on the route — any ground unit sees high-altitude traffic 48 hexes out, sensor stations and Mobile HQs twice that, and fighters see each other at 24. The gaps between pickets are routes. Intercepts plot ahead of the bandit's track — cleanly at LOCK, with drift below it, so jinking a poor track is a real escape. JOKER is the warning, BINGO forces the flight home — computed from your actual distance to base. Flights launch from bases or straight off a carrier's deck, fly their mission, and recover themselves into a free bay. DropShips are physical: they carry formations in bays (cargo rides along, invisible and fed by the ship), LIFT_OFF / LAND anywhere passable, and rearm their flights from ship stores. Spheroids crawl — 1 air hex per turn in atmosphere; their fast lane is ASCEND → cross in space → DESCEND. Aerodynes fly full speed. Recon sorties: a flight on a RECON order photographs a corridor 5 ground hexes wide under its track — terrain becomes scouted, formations under the camera get a passive detection roll per turn, and enemy bases are photographed outright (buildings can't dodge; once the photo is home they sit on your map permanently) — but the film rides home with the plane: reports deliver when it lands back inside the net, and are lost with the aircraft if it doesn't. Ground scouts log bases inside sensor range the same way, and a capital battery that fires marks its own silo for whoever it shot at. Flak: AA units umbrella their hex + 2; every launch, landing, or drop pass inside it takes one shot per battery — and the battery is graded by its real guns (a Partisan's LB-X mounts count double): more strength = lower to-hit, and a massed battery tears two damage steps per hit. Firing reveals the battery; flak batters but never one-shots. Anti-capital emplacements are the other tier entirely: a base mounting a real capital weapon at its printed Total Warfare stats — a TW high-altitude hex is 18 km, exactly one operational hex, so the ranges are 1:1. A Barracuda battery covers up to 50 air hexes; to-hit runs by range band, short 5+ out to extreme 11+. It fires on every DropShip that flies, lands, or drops inside the umbrella — one shot per battery per turn — and CAN kill outright (a Killer Whale is three damage steps). Their magazines run dry, and a battery is silenced the moment enemy ground troops stand on it: land outside the zone and march, or take the guns. Combat drops scatter 1d6 (piloting reduces it), worse in storms or through flak.

Space

The system map is nodes and lanes. Burns follow real physics — accelerate, flip, brake — and your drive plume is visible to everyone at the speed of light: they learn your tonnage and vector, delayed by the distance. Cold-coasting is quiet but slow. JumpShips recharge sails for days and are taboo to kill. Pirate points are secret, surveyed shortcuts. A false transponder squawk holds until someone inspects it — or until you maneuver like a warship.

Victory

Objectives accrue VP per day to whoever holds them (some are hidden; some are fake). First side to the campaign's threshold — or best total at the deadline — wins. Watch the war diary (📖 on your screen): it is your side's story so far.
